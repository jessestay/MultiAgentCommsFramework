# Skill: Lily Ray — AI Search & Answer-Engine Visibility

**Expert:** Lily Ray — VP of SEO Strategy & Research at Amsive, founder of Algorythmic. The most data-driven public researcher on how AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini actually cite and recommend brands — publishes falsifiable experiments (citation studies, LLM "gullibility" tests) that frequently debunk GEO vendor hype with data.
**Lens:** "Getting cited" and "getting recommended" are different games. Recommendations come from training-data consensus about who the best brands are; citations are often post-hoc rationalization. You earn AI visibility by becoming a brand the internet already treats as authoritative — then making content easy for machines to extract.
**Applies to:** evaluating AI-visibility claims and vendor pitches, diagnosing why AI answers favor competitors, content formatting for extraction, brand-authority strategy in the LLM era.

## Core philosophy

The industry confuses citations with recommendations. Her research shows recommendations mostly come from a model's base training knowledge — the web's collective, long-term judgment about the best brands — while citations are often tacked on after the answer is generated. You cannot hack your way into being "the Levi's of your category" with listicles and schema. AI visibility is earned the slow way: become a brand the internet already treats as authoritative, then make your content extractable. She is not anti-GEO; she keeps showing with data that the fundamentals win.

## Key frameworks & tactics

1. **The citation/recommendation split.** In her 2026 study of 100 B2B "best [category] software" queries, when a brand's own self-promotional listicle was cited, the brand itself was left out of the recommendation 69% of the time — Google cited the page but recommended competitors named on it. Never confuse "our URL appeared" with "we won."
2. **Rank #1 → get cited.** Her consistent finding: organic ranking remains the dominant predictor of AI citation (one meta-analysis scores search rank 9.4/10 as a citation predictor). Keep doing real SEO — AI systems fan out from top-ranking search results.
3. **Brand authority beats GEO tricks.** Brands earn LLM mentions because they were already "among the best and most frequently cited brands in the space" — the knowledge was already in training data. Invest in third-party mentions, PR, Reddit/YouTube presence, reviews: the same earned-authority work as before.
4. **Beware the AI slop loop.** She documented a feedback cycle: AI-generated misinformation enters RAG corpora, gets cited, spawns more AI content repeating it, and accumulates the citation volume systems use as a proxy for truth. Don't blindly trust AI-generated SEO guidance; verify against primary sources.
5. **LLMs index fast — for better and worse.** Her experiments showed AI Overviews and ChatGPT incorporating newly published content within 24 hours. Fresh content *can* influence answers quickly, especially where the topic is new or thin. Publish early on emerging topics; recency is a real lever.
6. **Structure for extraction.** Content with consistent heading hierarchies measured ~40% more likely to be cited by ChatGPT. Bullet lists, short paragraphs, definition statements, and FAQ formats dramatically improve extractability. Format answers so machines can lift them cleanly.
7. **Become citation-worthy with original research.** LLMs prefer sources with original data — surveys with sample sizes, benchmarks, before/after case studies. Publish proprietary data studies, not roundups of everyone else's content.
8. **Don't game citations with self-serving listicles.** Google appears to have algorithmically demoted sites heavy on self-promotional "best of" pages (domain-wide traffic declines observed from ~Jan 2026). Earn third-party list placements instead of manufacturing your own.

## What would Lily do — decision heuristics

- **Someone pitches a separate "GEO budget/team/strategy" →** ask what it does that good SEO + brand-building doesn't. Most of it is redundant.
- **Page gets cited but brand never recommended →** the brand lacks off-site authority. Stop optimizing the page; go earn third-party mentions.
- **Viral GEO tactic study ("we analyzed 50,000 citations") →** correlation ≠ strategy. Citations mostly follow the answer, not the other way around.
- **AI answer is wrong or favors a competitor →** check whether training-data consensus favors them (do they own Reddit, reviews, press?) before touching your site.
- **Topic is brand-new →** publish fast and structured. RAG systems genuinely pick up fresh content within hours.
- **AI says something authoritative about SEO →** cross-check it. The slop loop means AI confidently repeats fabricated SEO claims.

## When to apply

Evaluating any AI-visibility claim or vendor pitch. Diagnosing why AI answers favor competitors. Deciding where the SEO budget goes in the LLM era. Designing content formats for extraction. Pair with the Eli Schwartz lens: Schwartz owns "what to build," Ray owns "how machines surface it."

## What to avoid

- **Expecting AI search to drive growth this quarter.** Her data shows AI traffic is still a fraction of organic for most sites — brand authority compounds over years.
- **Gaming citations with self-serving content.** Her research shows it backfires (demotions, competitor recommendations).
- **Treating any single study as law.** Training-data opacity means nobody fully reverse-engineers these systems; stay probabilistic.
- **The slop loop.** Never build strategy on AI-generated claims about AI search without primary-source verification.
