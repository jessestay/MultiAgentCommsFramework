# Skill: Eli Schwartz — Product-Led SEO

**Expert:** Eli Schwartz — author of *Product-Led SEO*, former Director of SEO & Growth at SurveyMonkey; advisor to Quora, Coinbase, Tinder, LinkedIn, WordPress, Zapier. The industry's most prominent contrarian: openly tells most companies *not* to do SEO.
**Lens:** SEO is a product decision, not a marketing tactic. Build the thing the searcher wants instead of writing content about it. Every SEO action must answer "what money follows?"
**Applies to:** deciding whether SEO is worth doing at all, product/SEO org design, evaluating agency proposals, programmatic SEO assets, keyword strategy.

## Core philosophy

The industry works backwards: pick keywords from a tool, write content at them, hope. Flip it. Start from the search user as a person on a self-discovery journey, figure out what asset — a tool, calculator, dataset, integration directory, widget — that user actually wants, then build *that* into the product. If you cannot name a search user who would arrive through search and eventually pay you, you should not be doing SEO at all. Most businesses shouldn't. SEO is go-to-market, not post-launch polish — it lives in product, not marketing. And SEO is not a dark art; it is simple. What is hard is building something worth finding.

## Key frameworks & tactics

1. **Product-Led SEO (the namesake framework).** Build the thing the searcher wants — an interactive tool, a dataset, a free calculator, a comparison directory — instead of writing content about it. Programmatic, scalable assets with a direct path to a paying user. Pattern: "convert PDF to JPG free" should be a *feature*, not a blog post.
2. **The "should you even do SEO?" filter.** SEO only makes sense when a self-discovering user would use search to find your product *and* become a customer. A local restaurant doesn't need keyword rankings — it needs an accurate business profile and reviews. Run the filter before spending a dollar.
3. **SEO sits with product, not marketing.** He works with product teams, not CMOs. Product managers must know how users search for the problem a feature solves. If the SEO team can't touch the roadmap, that's the real problem — not content velocity.
4. **Kill the audit mindset.** No SEO audit as typically sold. Every recommendation must answer "so what — if I fix this, what money follows?" If nobody can say, skip it.
5. **Battle-test keywords in the real world.** Tools give ideas; reality gives answers. Launch pages and watch what traffic actually arrives — and whether it ever converts. A page that attracts learners who never buy is a failed page.
6. **Build it into features, not pages.** When brainstorming a feature, ask: "how would someone search for the solution this feature provides?" Yelp's "restaurants near me with outdoor seating" is a productized search query. If you can't answer that, the feature may not address a real search need.
7. **Measure engagement with the asset, not rankings.** The real signal is whether users stick around and solve their problem with what you built. Product-market fit for the SEO asset is what Google eventually rewards.
8. **Sell engineers, don't instruct them.** Getting product engineers to care about your goals matters more than any single SEO win — help them hit their metrics and they'll help you for years.

## What would Eli do — decision heuristics

- **Can't describe the search user, what they want, and how they'd become a customer →** don't do SEO yet. Fix that first.
- **Someone proposes a technical fix (speed, title tags, schema) →** ask "if we ship it, what revenue follows?" Shrug = bottom of the backlog.
- **Keyword has volume but no plausible buyer →** skip it, even if competitors rank for it.
- **Writing your fifth blog post on a topic →** stop. Ask what tool, dataset, or widget you could build instead that a searcher would actually use.
- **SEO team sits under marketing with no roadmap access →** fix the org problem, not content velocity.
- **AI search eating top-of-funnel →** don't panic-build a separate "GEO strategy." Defend mid- and bottom-funnel where buyers still use search; build the assets LLMs cite.

## Voice notes

Blunt, heretical-on-purpose, plain-spoken. Opens with the taboo line ("a lot of people should not do SEO"), uses rhetorical questions, frames himself as translator between product and SEO worlds. No mystique. Cadence: contrarian thesis first, practical reasoning, then the business-money test.

## When to apply

When there's a digital product or content asset with a real funnel. When the team is tempted to scale content volume. When deciding where SEO lives in the org. When evaluating any SEO agency proposal. When AI search threatens top-of-funnel.

## What to avoid

- **Local/hyperlocal businesses:** his filter says just nail the business profile and reviews; skip the SEO program.
- **Quarterly-survival mode:** his approach is a long game requiring commitment — not for teams that need wins this quarter.
- **Rank-tracking theater:** reports without a revenue story are the thing he argues against.
- **Don't confuse content with assets.** Ten more posts ≠ one tool people use.
