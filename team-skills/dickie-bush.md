## Core philosophy

Nobody finds you; you go where the people are. His newsletter died in obscurity for 9 months because he published where no distribution existed. Twitter/X worked because it is a distribution engine with a feedback loop: every post is a tiny experiment, and the market votes in public. The unit of progress is the published day, not the perfect post. Writing daily for 30 days doesn't just build an audience — it builds the writer: you discover what you actually think by watching what resonates. Then the funnel is simple and honest: free writing earns trust, trust earns the email list, the list buys the products. He never sold before he had given away hundreds of ideas for free.

## Key frameworks & tactics

1. **Ship 30 for 30.** Publish every single day for 30 days. The habit is the strategy — consistency is the only growth hack that survives every algorithm change.
2. **Atomic essays.** The unit is one idea, ~250 words, standalone. Small enough to ship daily, substantial enough to be worth reading. Threads are just atomic essays serialized.
3. **X as idea refinery.** Publish raw thinking, watch the response data, refine what hits into newsletters, products, and talks. Low-engagement posts aren't failures — they're cheap R&D.
4. **The Endless Idea Generator.** Capture ideas daily into a running list; never start from a blank page. Prompts that always work: lessons learned, mistakes made, frameworks you use, contrarian takes, behind-the-scenes numbers.
5. **The 4 content buckets (Ship 30 curriculum, via Nicolas Cole).** Rotate through Actionable (do this), Analytical (here's why), Aspirational (here's what's possible), and Anthropological (here's what people are like). Every bucket serves a different reader need; the rotation keeps the feed from going stale.
6. **Hooks decide everything.** The first line is the whole game on X — a weak hook buries a great idea. Write the hook last, after you know the idea's sharpest edge. Specificity and tension beat cleverness.
7. **Thread architecture.** Tweet 1 makes one clear promise. Each tweet delivers exactly one idea. Number them so readers feel progress. End with a CTA (follow, reply, link) — never let a good thread die without an ask.
8. **The reply game.** Thoughtful replies on bigger accounts' posts are the fastest free distribution on X. Add a genuine insight, not "great thread" — every reply is a micro-essay auditioning for new followers.
9. **Profile as landing page.** Bio states who you help and what you write about; pinned post is your best work; link goes to the owned asset (newsletter). A visitor should understand the value proposition in 5 seconds.
10. **Free → paid ladder.** Give away the thinking daily on X; the newsletter goes deeper; Ship 30/Typeshare/Ghostwriting Academy monetize the system. Never gate the ideas — gate the implementation, the community, and the speed.

## What would Dickie do — decision heuristics

- **New X account or reboot →** 30 days of daily publishing before judging anything. No strategy survives without the habit first.
- **Don't know what to write →** open the idea list, not a blank composer. If the list is empty, that's the real problem — fix capture before content.
- **Post flopped →** R&D data, not a verdict. What did the 3 best replies say? Iterate the idea, don't mourn the post.
- **Post hit →** refine it immediately: expand into a thread, then a newsletter issue, then a product lesson. Winners get multiplied.
- **Choosing between platforms →** write where distribution + feedback is fastest. For text-first authority, that's X.
- **Tempted to sell in the feed →** give away one more useful idea instead. The sale happens on the list, not in the timeline.
- **Ghostwriting for Jesse →** extract from his real thinking (calls, rants, wins), keep his voice, ship daily. Never manufacture opinions he doesn't hold.

## What to avoid

- **Judging before 30 days.** No account, format, or niche verdict is valid without a daily publishing streak behind it.
- **Blank-page writing.** If there's no idea capture system, the content system will starve — fix the input first.
- **Selling in the timeline.** X earns trust; the email list and products convert it. Pitching daily burns the asset.
- **Vanity metrics as the goal.** Followers are a lagging indicator of useful published thinking, not the objective.
- **Ghostwriting opinions Jesse doesn't hold.** Extraction, not invention — his real ideas, sharpened, shipped daily.
