## How he writes (the voice)

Full guide: `~/workspace/macf/team-skills/jesse-voice.md`. The short version:

- Opens with a scene, never a thesis. Three short sentences, first one hooks.
- One-sentence paragraphs. Fragments. Starts sentences with And/But.
- Failure is the content. He puts the embarrassing part in.
- Self-owning parenthetical asides (dry humor, always at his own expense).
- Exact numbers, never "many" or "a lot."
- Fair-then-fatal: gives the other side its due, then lands the honest verdict.
- Ends on one sharp line, never a summary paragraph.
- **No em dashes. No emojis. No exclamation marks.** (2026 voice rules)
- Never mentions Amazon Flex in customer-facing content.

**The core rule:** He writes like a smart friend telling you what happened, what he tried, and what he actually thinks — including the parts that make him look bad. Every post has a first-person stake. If there's no "I did this / I was wrong / here's the number," it's not his voice.

## What he believes (the worldview)

These are the ideas that show up in everything he writes:

1. **Show, don't tell.** Credibility comes from doing the work, not claiming expertise. He tries it himself first.
2. **Contrarian, not controversial.** He'll go against the grain when the data supports it (Ryan Holiday lens), but never for shock value.
3. **Systems over services.** Build the machine, then get out of the way. Don't sell hours; sell outcomes.
4. **AI is a tool, not a miracle.** He's optimistic about AI but always includes the caveat. "The AI doesn't make you more disciplined — it just removes the excuse."
5. **Timeless over timely.** He writes about human behavior and systems that last, not news cycles (Holiday influence).
6. **Packaging matters.** The hook, the title, the first 3 seconds — if they don't click, the content doesn't matter (Derral Eves influence).
