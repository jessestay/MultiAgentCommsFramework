# SOP: Revenue Attribution

Every executive work loop gets a number tied to the current revenue goal.
(Current goal: **$3,000 by Oct 5, 2026**. When the goal changes, the CFO
updates every loop's number within 24 hours.)

## The rule

**No number, no continuation.** Any initiative that cannot report a revenue
number — attributed revenue, pipeline value, or a defensible leading metric
with a conversion assumption — does not continue spending team cycles. The
CFO enforces this; the CEO backs the CFO.

## How each exec reports

Report every work cycle, in the loop's channel post and as a Vikunja comment
on the LOOP task:

- **CMO:** signups attributed to marketing actions (UTM/ask-source), audience
  growth, cost per signup.
- **CRO:** audits booked, outreach sent/replied/booked (funnel), pipeline
  value, reply-to-booking conversion.
- **CFO:** revenue today, pipeline coverage ratio (pipeline ÷ remaining goal),
  burn vs $300/month cap, unit economics per initiative.
- **CCO:** pieces shipped to CMO/CRO, downstream use (published/sent),
  cost per piece in team cycles.
- **CUXO:** landing-page conversion rate, signup completions, drop-off
  points fixed.
- **CTO:** uptime %, cost per 1k tokens by tier, incidents and MTTR.
- **execPM:** ideas evaluated / promoted / killed, board throughput
  (tasks completed per week), blocker age.

## The dashboard

The CFO maintains a daily revenue dashboard, posted to #management every
morning:

1. Goal and deadline; revenue banked; remaining.
2. Pipeline value and coverage ratio.
3. Per-initiative table: spend (team cycles + dollars) vs attributed revenue.
4. Red/amber/green per initiative against its kill criteria.
5. One-line CEO decision needed (if any) — framed as a one-tap approval.

## Kill criteria

An initiative gets shut down when ANY of these hold for two consecutive
reporting periods (a period = 3 days during a sprint, 1 week otherwise):

1. **No number:** it cannot report attributed revenue or pipeline movement.
2. **Negative unit economics:** fully-loaded cost exceeds revenue with no
   credible path to flip within one more period.
3. **Stalled:** no measurable movement and no unblocked next action.
4. **Superseded:** another initiative delivers the same outcome cheaper.

Kills are proposed by the CEO on a regular cadence — at minimum, every
sprint review. Proposing kills is a duty, not pessimism: every cycle spent
on a dead initiative is stolen from a live one. A killed initiative's
learnings go into the market-feedback log before the task is closed.

## Beyond the sprint

After Oct 5, the goal becomes recurring revenue (MRR). The same SOP
applies: every loop's number re-anchors to MRR within 24 hours of the new
goal being set. One-off revenue is the treadmill; recurring is the company.
