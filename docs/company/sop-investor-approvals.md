# SOP: Investor Approvals (approval-burden reduction)

Jesse (investor/chairman) is the bottleneck on spend, send, and publish.
This SOP makes his approval instant instead of a queue.

## Principles

1. He sees outcomes and decisions, never tasks or process.
2. Every request is a SINGLE one-tap decision: approve or reject, with the
   exact action pre-built.
3. Requests are batched where possible — one DM with three decisions beats
   three DMs.
4. Pre-gated quality bar: nothing reaches him that the team hasn't already
   vetted.

## The pre-gate (before anything reaches Jesse)

- [ ] Draft is complete and final — no "rough draft, tell me what you think."
- [ ] Content passes `content_gate2` where applicable (18 rules + positive
      control). Holds still require his explicit release.
- [ ] Facts verified; no placeholders; links work.
- [ ] The exact send/publish/buy action is specified (where, when, how much).
- [ ] Reversible? If yes, say so — it lowers the decision cost.

## The one-tap format

Every approval request follows this shape, in one message:

> **Decision:** [one line — what you're approving]
> **Action on your yes:** [exactly what the team will do, no further input]
> **Cost/risk:** [money, reputation, reversibility — one line]
> **Reply YES or NO.**

No backstory. No options menu. No "let me know what you prefer."

## Batching

Non-urgent approvals accumulate into a maximum of TWO batches per day
(morning digest, evening digest). Urgent items (money expiring, time-critical
launches) go immediately, one DM, same format.

## Turnaround target

- Urgent: decision within 2 hours.
- Batched: decision by next digest.
- Anything waiting >24 hours is escalated by the CEO with a default-action
  recommendation ("if I don't hear by X, I will <safe default>") — the
  default must be the reversible, draft-preserving option.

## Metric: approval latency

The CFO tracks, per week:

- Median hours from request to decision.
- % decided within target.
- Requests bounced back for incompleteness (target: zero — the pre-gate
  should catch everything).

A rising bounce rate means the team is pushing unfinished work upward —
that's a team defect, not an investor problem.
